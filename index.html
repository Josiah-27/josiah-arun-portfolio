04 — Hardware Lab

Smart Room Automation, built from pure logic

A bidirectional people counter built from TTL logic chips, with no microcontroller. It counts people walking in and out, shows the room's occupancy, and switches the light and fan for you. Try the live simulation, which runs the same flip-flop logic as the real circuit.

Live logic simulation 74LS74 → 74LS192 → 74LS47

Light OFF

Room empty: light and fan are OFF. Press “Person enters” to walk through the beams.

  1. Stage 01

    Sensing

    Two 650 nm laser beams fall on two LDRs. When a person blocks a beam, the LDR's resistance jumps and a BC547 switch outputs a clean TTL signal.

    Laser + LDR + BC547
  2. Stage 02

    Direction detection

    The beams sit closer together than a person is wide. Flip-flops capture which beam broke first: A then B is an entry, B then A is an exit.

    74LS74 + 74LS08
  3. Stage 03

    Counting & display

    Entry pulses drive the UP input and exit pulses the DOWN input of a BCD counter. A decoder shows the count on a seven-segment display.

    74LS192 + 74LS47
  4. Stage 04

    Zero detection

    The counter outputs Q0–Q3 are ORed together. The result is HIGH whenever at least one person is inside the room.

    74LS32
  5. Stage 05

    Load control

    The zero-detect signal switches the light and fan. A weighted resistor network raises the fan's drive voltage as the count rises.

    Weighted R + driver

Results & next steps

Tested by walking through the beams in both directions: entries add one, exits subtract one, and the loads switch off the moment the last person leaves.

Strong ambient light on the LDRs and two people crossing side by side are the main sources of error. One 74LS192 counts 0–9; cascading counters extends the range, and relays would let the circuit drive mains appliances.
